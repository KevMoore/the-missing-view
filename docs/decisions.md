# The Missing View — Decision Record

Status: locked from the CTO grilling session, 2026-08-29.
These decisions override the PRD wherever they differ. Divergences are marked.

## Play mode

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| D1  | **Both, as one build.** A facilitator picks "everyone in one room" or "everyone on a call" and the instructions change; the game does not. Phones always connected over a WebSocket from anywhere, so remote needed a shareable invite link and the right words, not an architecture. Debate happens face to face or in the call — the game never carried voice. | Closes the divergence from **PRD §13** ("designed specifically for remote/online group play"). What remote still lacks is not code: the facilitator must share the screen tab _with sound_, or the room sees the art and hears neither the score nor the suspects. |
| D2  | **No microphone, no recording.**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Follows from D1. Kills the transcript, so see D9.                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| D3  | **Join with a room code + first name.** No player accounts. Facilitator is the only account holder. Opt-in email capture after the reveal to receive the private read.                                                                                                                                                                                           | Under ten seconds to join. No IT involvement.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| D4  | **Facilitator does not play.** They run the session from a console.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | A team of six needs a seventh body. Accepted.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

## Game design

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                         | Notes                                                                                                                      |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D5  | **Three timed acts, each ending in a forced team commitment.** Act 1 gather (~15m), Act 2 pressure (~20m), Act 3 commit (~15m).  | Guarantees pacing, gives the eight team moments somewhere to live.                                                         |
| D6  | **Clues are private until tabled.** Tabling is an explicit, credited act. Private whispers to one player are allowed.            | The evidence board is the team's shared brain, built by named people.                                                      |
| D7  | **Interrogation: anyone submits a question from their phone; it is queued, then answered on the big screen for the whole room.** | Heads stay up. We still know who asked what.                                                                               |
| D8  | **Final accusation is a single team answer.** No private pre-vote.                                                               | CTO call. Cost accepted: we lose the private-vs-public judgement gap. Partially recovered from act-end commitment changes. |
| D18 | **Case one: 1920s country house, full period piece.**                                                                            | CTO call. Cost accepted: period authoring burden, costume-party brand risk.                                                |

## Insight engine

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                         | Notes                                                           |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D9  | **Signals are phone moves only.** If a behaviour matters, it has a button: table, whisper, challenge, back a theory, ask a suspect, nominate, change position at an act break.                   | Fully deterministic and explainable. No audio.                  |
| D10 | **Scoring is deterministic; the LLM only writes the prose.** Every line of the reveal cites a real logged act with a timestamp.                                                                  | The model never decides who you are.                            |
| D11 | **Visibility.** Big screen: one named strength per player, with evidence. Player's phone: the fuller private read. Facilitator: team shape and blind spots only — **never per-person profiles.** | Protects the room. Keeps us out of "HR assessment" positioning. |

## LLM

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Notes                                                                                  |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D12 | LLM plays the suspects, writes the reveal, and acts as game master for pacing nudges.                                                                                                                                                           |                                                                                        |
| D13 | **The model is never told the solution.** Each suspect prompt carries only `knows` / `believes` / `hides` / `lies_about` for that character. Replies are checked against the case's `forbidden_facts` before display, and regenerated on a hit. | Containment by construction, not by instruction. Immune to prompt injection by design. |
| D14 | **Mysteries are generated by an authoring tool, never at runtime.** LLM drafts → automatic validator → human review → published case. Runtime loads published cases only.                                                                       |                                                                                        |
| D15 | **Every published case ships a pre-generated answer bank.** On timeout or network loss the server serves the nearest banked answer.                                                                                                             | Venue wifi is the top session-killing risk. Also caps cost and latency.                |

## Content model

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D16 | **Clues are a pool with ownership constraints, dealt at start for the actual head count.** Validator proves solvable and fair for n = 4..8. Late arrivals are dealt in.                                                                                                                                       | No session ever fails to start because someone is off sick.                                                                                                                                          |
| D17 | Validator gates on: exactly one solution; the solution needs clues from ≥3 different holders; **every player who can hold a probative clue does** — `min(playerCount, provenBy.length)`, checked at every head count and seed; no player can solve it alone; all eight team moments present; no orphan clues. | This validator is the core IP. The "every player" rule was documented from the start but only implemented on 2026-08-30; until then the deal gave the whole proof to seats 0, 1 and 2 at every seed. |

## Tech and commercials

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D19 | **TypeScript end to end.** React + Vite clients (screen, phone, facilitator console), Node server, WebSockets, Postgres. Monorepo with a shared `case-schema` package.                                                                                                                                                                                              | Chosen over .NET/SignalR specifically so the case schema cannot drift between authoring, validation, server and clients.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| D20 | **Server-authoritative.** All rules and all model calls server-side. Clients render state and send intents.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| D21 | **Security light for the MVP**, but room codes are unguessable, dossiers are never sent to the wrong phone, and the solution never reaches a client before the reveal.                                                                                                                                                                                              |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| D22 | **No payments in the MVP.** Facilitator accounts created by hand; paid pilots invoiced off-platform.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Success signal: a facilitator we have never met runs a session that lands, and books another.                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| D33 | **An act break is a card, not a number changing.** Act title, its opening narrated by the same voice as the prologue, a recap of the last decision and the strongest theory, and a countdown. It holds for as long as the narration takes, falling back to twenty seconds.                                                                                          | Ten seconds is a good pause and a poor read — the openings run to forty-five words, which is eighteen seconds spoken. The recap is the part nobody holds in their head after twenty minutes of argument.                                                                                                            |
| D34 | **Pacing nudges are deterministic and never hand anything over (D12).** In the last five minutes of an act, if nothing has happened for ninety seconds, the screen names a clue somebody is still holding — never its contents, never who holds it. That player's own phone is told privately. Once per act.                                                        | A room that has to ask "who has the grey thread?" is doing the thing the game is about. Naming the holder on a shared screen would be the opposite of D11.                                                                                                                                                          |
| D32 | **Results across sessions live at `/insights`, and the page does not exist without `TMV_INSIGHTS_KEY`.** One number gets the top of the page — the share who did not see it coming — with a plain-language reading beside it. A missing measurement renders as "—", never as zero.                                                                                  | Metrics you cannot read are metrics you do not have (D30). Session data should not be a public URL even under D21's "security light", and a percentage nobody interprets the same way twice is worse than no percentage.                                                                                            |
| D31 | **Decision making is read separately from contribution (PRD §6).** Five styles — decisive, open to being wrong, considered, independent-minded, consensus-building — from the three forced commitments. Private to the player, never on the shared screen, and never phrased as a failing.                                                                          | §6 asks for four categories and three were covered; the fourth had the cleanest data in the game and was thrown away. Changing your mind in public outranks everything else because it is the rarest and hardest.                                                                                                   |
| D30 | **The game measures itself (PRD §19).** Every finished session persists metrics derived from the move stream and logs a one-line summary, and each player is asked three questions plus one optional line — the first being whether they knew it was about the team before the reveal.                                                                              | §19 calls that "a particularly important metric" and it was the one thing nothing captured. A room that got the culprit wrong having reached seven moments beat one that guessed right in twenty minutes, and none of that was visible. Three taps because a room that has just finished will not fill in a survey. |
| D29 | **The eight team moments are measured and reported.** A moment is _offered_ when its clue reaches the board, credited to whoever tabled it, and _lands_ when someone else acts on it within three minutes. The room sees what it reached, by name. The facilitator also sees what was offered and passed over, and what never happened at all — and no names (D11). | The moments were authored on every clue and gated on by D17 from the start, and no runtime code read them. The game validated on a team-development vocabulary it did not use. "Someone did synthesis and the room talked straight past it" is the finding a group cannot get for itself.                           |
| D28 | **A drafted case is filled onto the skeleton of one that already validates.** The model writes prose; the arithmetic — who is guilty, which clue rules whom out, what shares a hand — is lifted from a working case and reapplied. The clue stage sees suspects by number only.                                                                                     | A model is very good at prose and very bad at arithmetic it cannot see. This way a draft satisfies D17 and D27 by construction, and the validator checks craft rather than tossing a coin.                                                                                                                          |
| D27 | **A case must be provable, not merely suggestive.** Clues declare `implicates` and `exonerates`. The validator applies every probative clue's exclusions and requires exactly one suspect to survive; no single clue may leave one standing; no clue may sit in the proof doing nothing; and no player may hold enough to rule out everyone alone.                  | Every other rule checks shape. A draft can pass them all and be seven clues that read as damning and prove nothing — which is exactly the mistake a generated case makes, and the reason this rail exists before any generation does.                                                                               |
| D26 | **Act 1 never names the culprit.** It names other suspects, and poses questions later acts answer. Act 2 turns toward the culprit; act 3 proves it. Enforced by test, not by good intentions.                                                                                                                                                                       | The diary once read "E.C. — midnight", and exactly one suspect had those initials: the case was solvable in twenty seconds from one act-1 clue.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| D24 | **The opening is painted stills, not 3D.** A slow push, a long cross-dissolve, a serif caption, CSS only. The facilitator plays it; the screen ends it.                                                                                                                                                                                                             | WebGL is a failure mode on a borrowed venue laptop, and 3D primitives fight an oil-paint art direction.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| D25 | **Suspects speak, and are cast in the case pack.** `voice` picks the synthesised voice; `voiceDirection` describes the sound, separately from `persona`, which describes the writing.                                                                                                                                                                               | ~$0.004 an answer. Text reaches the screen first and the voice follows, so nobody waits on silence.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| D23 | **`/` serves two audiences, told apart by the QR code's `?code=` parameter.** With a code, it is the join form alone. Without one, it is the front door: premise, join, run a game, and how it works.                                                                                                                                                               | A fourth route would split the story and drift. The QR must never pay for the landing page — asserted in `e2e/landing.spec.ts`.                                                                                                                                                                                     |

## The team, and the teams

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D36 | **The accusation is the house's, not a player's.** There is no move that makes one. Each player commits to a name; the accusation exists only once every player who can commit has committed to the **same** name. Unanimity, not a majority. Anyone may withdraw until it locks, everyone can see where the house stands as it forms, and the motive is written by anyone rather than voted on. Bots investigate but do not decide, so they are not counted. | It was one tap by whoever got there first, which made the ending a race in a game about a team deciding (D8, PRD §6). A majority would have been worse than a race: it lets a team accuse over the head of somebody who disagrees, and the disagreement is the most interesting thing on the table. A hidden tally would let the accusation land on people who never saw it coming.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| D37 | **The facilitator casts the players.** Any seat left blank is still cast at random from what is left, around what is taken. The console shows the whole cast as painted cards, each with its part and its vocal direction.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | They know the people in the room, and the game is about how those people work together. The vocal direction is on the card because it is the one field that states sex and age plainly — the game reads a player's questions aloud in their character's voice, and a mismatch was reaching the room as a suspect calling a man "Madam".                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| D38 | **A house IS a `GameState`.** One or two houses per session; two play the same case side by side, share nothing, and are compared at the end. `Room` owns what they share — the code, the sockets, the big screen, the clock; `Table` owns one house. Interrogation history is per house. **A screen belongs to a house (D40).** How close a house is to accusing is shown as a count, never a name.                                                          | The alternative was a `teamId` on every player, clue, board entry, theory, question, commitment and accusation, plus a filter at every read: a change touching every line of the engine, which can be got wrong quietly — one unfiltered read and the competition is over. This way the leak is impossible to write. Interrogation history is per house for the same reason — the suspects' replies are spoken aloud through the room's one set of speakers, and both houses are sitting in it. Suspects are questioned in front of everybody, as they would be in the drawing room. The **history** is still per house, so one house's line of questioning does not steer the answers the other one gets. |
| D39 | **Deliberation is excluded wherever activity is measured.** `commit-vote`, `accuse-commit` and `accuse-withdraw` do not count as responses to a tabled clue, and do not count toward a player's share of the work.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | D36 made a unanimous accusation up to eight moves inside a few seconds. Counted, they land inside the response window of every act-3 clue and make every act-3 team moment "land" for free (D29), and they tell the facilitator at the debrief that whoever tapped fastest dominated the room (D30).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| D40 | **A big screen belongs to one house, and the server sends it nothing else.** With two houses, a screen joins as `/screen?code=X&house=h1` or is asked which house it is for, and shows only that house's questions, theories and board. A facilitator may ask for both, on their own monitor. Filtered on the server, not in the browser.                                                                                                                     | Two houses were sealed off from each other in the engine and then put back together on one display: the split screen showed each team the other's evidence, which is the one thing the head-to-head cannot allow. Two teams playing head to head want two screens — and a view that reaches a client has reached it, whatever the CSS does about it afterwards (D20). The console hands out one link per house and says why.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
